Added mass per satellite: full-scale hardening (whole body, 11.7 m²) 234 kg (+78%); layered: vault 10.2 kg (0.36 m² + 3 kg housing/pump) + 3 kg other = 13.2 kg (+4%)
Deploying at 300 km instead of 210 km: 53 m/s more from the upper stage -> 1.9% less payload per launch (each satellite then needs about 1 kg less of its own fuel to climb)

Per satellite: full-scale hardening $1.58M (range 0.85-4.17M), layered $83k (40-166k)
Per year (800 new satellites on 16 launches):
   full-scale radiation hardening  : $  1268M  (range 681-3336M), 1.27% of the $100 billion system per year
   our layered protection          : $    80M  (range 40-161M), 0.08% of the $100 billion system per year
   layered / full: 6%  (5%-6% across the range)

What one lost batch costs (50 satellites + their launch): $78M (range 48-150M)
   The layered protection for a whole year costs as much as losing 1.0 batches (range 0.8-1.1).
   With 2022 practice (deploy at 210 km whatever the forecast), in a year like 2035 58% of 5-day windows after a launch contain a G1+ storm: about 9 of the 16 batches a year would meet one in their first days, and on every 2035 day the air at 210 km is denser than on the day of the February 2022 losses (drag.py).
   So the layers pay for themselves if they save about one batch a year. Full-scale hardening costs as much as losing 16 batches a year and would not have saved the February 2022 satellites: they were lost to drag, not radiation.
